The style

Drawn by hand, on purpose.

Minimal line work and halftone texture, all in black and white, so it drops into a brand without fighting it. The range covers technology, AI, outdoor life, work, and a set of rough scribbles — arrows, circles, stars — for when a page needs a mark rather than a picture.

None of it is generated. That is the point. A model trained on everyone’s work averages out into something you have already seen. These were drawn slowly, one at a time, and they look like it.

It also explains the pricing. Flattening a drawing to PNG costs me nothing, so that part is free. The layered original is the thing that took the hours.

Questions

The short version

Is the PNG really free for commercial work?

Yes. Client work, products you sell, ads, merchandise, print. No fee, no licence to buy, no attribution enforced — though credit is welcome, and it’s written into each file’s metadata if you never get around to it.

What you can’t do is resell the artwork itself as a pack or template, or claim you drew it.

So what am I actually paying for?

The vector. A PNG is a flat grid of pixels — you can place it, and that’s about it. The SVG still has every path separate: change the line weight, recolour it to your brand, delete the background, keep one figure and drop the rest, scale it to a billboard without it going soft.

If you only need to drop a picture into a slide, take the PNG and keep your money.

Why the name Pay for Layers?

Because that has always been the deal, and now it’s also the pricing page. The flat image is free; the layers cost money. Cheaper than explaining a tier system.

One payment, or a subscription?

One payment. You get every SVG in the library, and every illustration I add later, at no extra cost. There is no renewal and nothing to cancel.

How do I know these aren’t AI-generated?

Open an SVG in Illustrator or Figma and look at the paths. They are drawn, not traced from a raster — the line weights vary the way a hand varies, and the halftone is placed rather than filtered. If you want to see the process, the portfolio has more of it.

What can I open the files with?

PNG opens anywhere. SVG works in Figma, Illustrator, Inkscape, and Affinity, and drops straight into HTML, React, Keynote, and Google Slides. Illustrator and Inkscape handle the halftone texture more comfortably than Figma does, but all of them work.

Can I request an illustration?

Ask. If it fits the set, I’ll draw it — and buyers get it free, since the pack keeps growing. If it’s specific to your product, that’s paid work, and it should be, because then it’s only yours.
